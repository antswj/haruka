# Deliverable 3 — Merged verdict

**Hybrid, weighted heavily toward building our own — but the split is not even across the four lists, and pretending otherwise would be the mistake.**

Three of the four lists have no prior art at all: there is no downloadable クッション言葉 dataset, no 曖昧語辞書, and no Japanese task-verb or output-format-marker lexicon anywhere — not on GitHub, not in the SudachiDict/MeCab ecosystem, not from NINJAL. That is not a gap in the search; it is the actual state of the field, and it means D1's 247 hand-sourced entries are the asset, not a stopgap. The one list where adoption genuinely wins is the hedge family (〜かもしれない, 〜と思われる, 〜のではないか), where Tsutsuji's 16,801 functional expressions across 9 abstraction levels would take months to reproduce by hand — so take it, in a separate CC BY-SA 4.0 directory with attribution, and keep our code MIT. Around that, adopt three MIT/Apache pieces that solve problems adjacent to the lists rather than the lists themselves: SudachiDict's normalized forms for 表記ゆれ (load-bearing — the cushion list alone surfaced nine attested orthographic pairs like お手隙／お手すき and 添えず／沿えず, and naive string matching will miss half of them), the textlint-ja rule family as MIT-licensed prior art that already implements a meaningful fraction of this lint, and SNOW T15 under CC BY 4.0 as a minable source of verbose→plain rewrite pairs for the suggestion text.

**The honest tradeoffs.** *Coverage:* our lists are broad but shallow-tailed — good on the high-frequency forms every Japanese source names, weak on the long tail, and four candidate cushion-word pages were robots-blocked so some conventional phrases are certainly missing. Worse, the highest-yield finding in the vague list is a *pattern*, not a word: `[主観的形容詞]な感じで` generates unbounded surface forms (ラグジュアリーな感じで, シュッとした感じで) that no static list will ever catch, so a wordlist-only implementation will underperform regardless of how many entries it has. *Maintenance:* the lists are small enough to hand-maintain and will not drift the way a scraped corpus would, but every entry's provenance is a blog or a business-manner site, and those rot — the SlothLib stopword list in D2 is exactly this failure mode, its canonical host now returning DNS errors and the data surviving only in gists of unknown provenance. Versioning our own file avoids that. *License:* building our own is the only path that is unambiguously clean. Every substantial existing Japanese lexicon carries a condition — Tsutsuji is ShareAlike, WordNet demands notices on all copies, UniDic requires electing the BSD arm and avoiding every classical variant, and three of the four sentiment tools surveyed wear an MIT or BSD badge over vendored data that was never licensed for redistribution at all. That last pattern is the real risk to a public repo, and it argues for a rule: nothing gets vendored on the strength of a wrapper's LICENSE file.

**One caveat on our own data that should reach RUBRIC.md rather than being quietly smoothed over.** Sources genuinely disagree in 39 places, and two of those disagreements are structural rather than cosmetic. 適切に is simultaneously an NG prompt word (AI Academy) and the standard politeness upgrade that 言い換え guides recommend for ざっくり／適当な (Forbes, Oggi) — meaning a de-vaguing rewrite can produce text that trips our own rule, so 適切に must never be offered as a fix. And three seed words from the original brief — サクッと, ちゃちゃっと, まるっと — could not be corroborated by *any* Japanese source that treats them as problematic; Japanese dictionaries define them neutrally as colloquial manner adverbs. They are retained at `confidence: low` with explicit disagreement notes rather than dropped, because the decision to ship or cut them should be visible. Firing them at the same severity as いい感じに would not be defensible from the evidence.
